# Latent context, small chat models, and the J-space on GPT-2

*A reading note written 2026-09-04 for TC, the operator, in answer to four questions asked in session: whether a chat-trained version of GPT-2 exists; how far the small modern chat models (Qwen, Gemma and Llama at under two billion parameters) differ from GPT-2, and whether this project's harnesses could be turned over to one; whether the method of Anthropic's J-space paper can find "orthogonal context" inside GPT-2's internal state; and where, relative to the ordinary flow of that internal state, the paper's injected "thoughts" sit, and how they were discovered. It sits beside [JSPACE_PRIMER.md](JSPACE_PRIMER.md), which explains the paper itself, and does not repeat it.*

> **Provenance.** Facts about this project were read from the committed record: [FINDINGS.md](FINDINGS.md) findings F11 and F16, and, in the Stage 2 repository `ATR_research`, the operator report of 2026-07-31, the identifier register, and the Medium lens results. Facts about the paper were read from its web page and checked against the primer. Architecture numbers were read from the TransformerLens 3.8.1 source and from the models' published configuration files. The list of pre-fitted lenses was read from the Hugging Face repository `neuronpedia/jacobian-lens` through its file-listing interface on the day of writing. Nothing here was run: this note contains no new measurements, and each claim is marked as established (read from a record or a paper), inferred (reasoned from established facts), or speculation.

## 1. The answers in brief

**A chat-trained GPT-2 exists, though not from OpenAI.** The LaMini-GPT family (MBZUAI, 2023) is GPT-2 Small, Large and XL fine-tuned to follow written instructions, and its smallest member loads into this project's existing scripts with no code change, because its architecture, tokenizer and weight layout are exactly those of `gpt2`. Section 3 has the details.

**The small modern chat models are the same kind of object as GPT-2 with four parts changed and about a thousand times more training.** The four parts are how position is encoded, how activations are normalised, the shape of the feed-forward block, and how attention keys are shared between heads. The loop in `atr_engine.py` ports with a configuration change, because the named points at which it reads and writes the model's state are the same for every model the TransformerLens library loads. What breaks is not the code but three of this project's conventions: the injection point no longer erases position, the loudness convention lands at a very different multiple of natural strength, and the first token carries activations large enough to dominate a whole-tensor rescale. Section 4 has the table and the hazards.

**The paper's method applies to GPT-2, and this project has already applied it twice,** once as a restricted pilot on GPT-2 Small and once as a full fit on GPT-2 Medium. The new fact is that a pre-fitted, full-vocabulary lens for GPT-2 Small is now published by Neuronpedia, so the planned overlap test on Small no longer needs a seven-hour refit. Section 5 has the details.

**The "thoughts" are ordinary directions inside the model's residual stream, in the middle band of layers, not a separate channel and not orthogonal to the normal flow.** They were found by asking which directions, if nudged, most change what the model would say now or later, averaged over a thousand contexts. The workspace properties were, in the authors' own words, a surprise found afterwards. Section 5 has the details.

## 2. Where this project sits in the latent-reasoning literature

The survey the question cites, "A Survey on Latent Reasoning" (Zhu and thirty-two co-authors, arXiv 2507.06203, July 2025), collects methods in which a model carries out several steps of reasoning in its own internal numbers rather than in written-out words. Its sorting principle is where the repetition happens. Vertical methods send a layer's output back through the stack again: looped and universal transformers reuse the same layers several times; Coconut feeds the model's final hidden state back in as its next input; CoTFormer and Huginn do versions of the same with a depth that varies per token. Horizontal methods carry a state along the sequence instead: linear attention, state-space models, and test-time training, which updates a small set of weights as the text is read. A third family trains the repetition in, either by removing written reasoning steps during fine-tuning (the stepwise internalisation of Deng and colleagues) or by distilling a written chain of thought into a few continuous vectors (CODI). A fourth treats diffusion language models, which revise a whole text at once over many rounds, as reasoning of unbounded depth.

Two facts from that literature bear on this project.

First, GPT-2 Small is the standard small testbed in it, not a relic. Coconut, CODI, and both of Deng and colleagues' implicit-chain-of-thought papers fine-tuned GPT-2. On GSM8k, a benchmark of grade-school arithmetic word problems, CODI reports 43.7 percent accuracy with a 124-million-parameter GPT-2 against 34.1 percent for Coconut on the same model, and reports that this matches a GPT-2 that writes its reasoning out in words. That is established from the CODI paper. Working on GPT-2 and Pythia does not cut this project off from the latent-reasoning work; it puts the project on the same model the field uses.

Second, this project's loop is Coconut's latent mode with the training taken away. Coconut takes the last layer's hidden state at the final position, appends it to the input as if it were the next token's embedding, and trains the model in stages to make use of the extra passes. The loop in `atr_engine.py` takes the whole final residual tensor at every position, rescales it to a fixed loudness, overwrites the input to layer 0 with it, and trains nothing. The two differ in three ways: last position against all positions, append against overwrite, trained against frozen. On the survey's account, the difference in what comes out, a search over reasoning states in Coconut against a small set of settled states here, is the training. That is an inference, not a measurement. It points at one experiment: fine-tune GPT-2 Small with Coconut's public code, then run this project's loop and its lens on the result, to see whether a model trained to use its own recurrence moves its settled states into or out of the verbalizable directions described in section 5. The companion repository `ATR_plasticity` is one step along that road already, since it lets one weight matrix move under the loop, but with no task and no target.

## 3. Chat-trained GPT-2 and Pythia

No official chat GPT-2 exists. OpenAI released GPT-2 in 2019 as a plain text-continuation model and never released an instruction-following or conversational version. The model called "gpt2-chatbot" that appeared on the LMSYS Chatbot Arena in spring 2024 was an OpenAI test of a GPT-4-class model under a misleading name, later confirmed as such by OpenAI, and shares nothing with GPT-2's weights. Both facts are established.

Three community lineages fill the gap.

- **LaMini-GPT** (MBZUAI, 2023) is GPT-2 Small, Large and XL, at 124 million, 774 million and 1.5 billion parameters, fine-tuned on 2.58 million instruction-and-response pairs that were themselves generated by a larger model. Each answers a request written in plain English, wrapped in a fixed prompt template that the model card specifies. Architecture, tokenizer and weight layout are unchanged from `gpt2`, `gpt2-large` and `gpt2-xl`. This is established from the model cards.
- **DialoGPT** (Microsoft, 2019) uses GPT-2's architecture at 117, 345 and 762 million parameters, trained on 147 million exchanges from Reddit. It continues a conversation in the register of Reddit; it does not follow instructions. This is established from the paper.
- **Assorted Alpaca-style fine-tunes** on Hugging Face, the public model repository, of uneven and mostly undocumented quality. Two found in search are `Arjun-G-Ravi/chat-GPT2` and `suriya7/ChatGPT-2.V2`, the second itself built on LaMini-GPT-774M.

For Pythia the equivalents are large. Dolly v2 (Databricks, 2023) at 3, 7 and 12 billion parameters sits on Pythia-2.8B, 6.9B and 12B, and OpenAssistant released supervised fine-tunes on Pythia-12B. Nothing official exists at this project's 160-million and 410-million sizes. This is established.

What this buys the project is a post-trained twin of its base model at zero porting cost. The pilot lens script, `experiments/gpt2_small/05_jlens_pilot.py`, already loads GPT-2 through the `hf_model=` argument of TransformerLens, the interpretability library this project uses to load models and reach inside them, so that weights from an offline mirror can run under the `gpt2` configuration. The same call with LaMini-GPT-124M's weights in place of the mirror's gives a model with the same named read-and-write points, the same tokenizer and the same loop, so every existing script runs on it unchanged. The paper's section 6 is exactly this comparison, a base model against its post-trained version, and reports that post-training changes what the workspace holds (it acquires the Assistant's point of view) and not whether the workspace exists. What to expect of the twin's ability is inferred from its size, not measured: it will answer a one-line request in a sentence or two, and it will not reliably follow an instruction of the form "think about X while doing Y", which is the form the paper's instruction-dependent experiments need. Section 5.4 separates those experiments from the ones that need no instruction at all.

## 4. Small modern chat models against GPT-2, and what porting the harness entails

### 4.1 The architectures side by side

The table lists the models most likely to matter, with GPT-2 Small and Pythia-410m as the reference rows. "Width" is the number of numbers in the residual stream per token position, the running vector that every layer reads and adds to. "Key-value heads" is how many separate sets of attention keys the heads share; when it is smaller than the head count, several heads read the same keys, which saves memory and is called grouped-query attention. "Feed-forward block" is the part of each layer that is not attention; GELU and SwiGLU are two forms of the nonlinearity inside it, and "gated" is explained below the table. "Pre-fitted lens" means a Jacobian lens for that model is downloadable from `neuronpedia/jacobian-lens` without fitting one. All architecture numbers are established from the TransformerLens 3.8.1 source or the model's configuration file; the training-token counts are the developers' published figures.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| `pythia-410m` | 24 | 1,024 | 16 (16) | rotary, on a quarter of each head | LayerNorm, attention and feed-forward in parallel | dense, GELU | 50,304 | 300 billion tokens of The Pile | yes | no (only `pythia-70m-deduped`) |

Also relevant: `google/gemma-2-2b` and its instruction-tuned form load in TransformerLens and have pre-fitted lenses, at 26 layers and width 2,304. SmolLM2 is absent from the supported list of TransformerLens 3.8.1; the library's newer bridge loader may wrap it, which this note did not test.

Four changes recur down the table, and it helps to name what each one is. Rotary position encoding rotates each attention query and key by an angle set by its position, inside every attention layer, instead of adding a learned position vector to the input once. RMSNorm divides a vector by its root-mean-square size without subtracting its mean and has no bias term, where GPT-2's LayerNorm does both. A gated feed-forward block computes two linear projections of its input, multiplies them together after passing one through a nonlinearity, and only then projects back down; GPT-2's block has one projection up, a GELU, and one projection down. Grouped-query attention was defined above the table. Beyond architecture, the training budgets differ by roughly three orders of magnitude: GPT-2 saw on the order of ten billion tokens, and these models saw two to thirty-six trillion, which is why a 270-million-parameter Gemma answers questions that a 124-million-parameter GPT-2 cannot. That the extra training changes the model's iterated dynamics or its workspace structure is speculation until measured.

### 4.2 What porting the harness entails

The loop in `atr_engine.py` reads the residual stream at the point named `blocks.{layer_end}.hook_resid_post`, rescales it, writes it to the point named `blocks.{layer_start}.hook_resid_pre`, and decodes through the final normalisation `ln_final` and the unembedding matrix `W_U`, the matrix that turns a final-layer vector into a score for every vocabulary token. Those names are identical for every model in the table, because TransformerLens gives every architecture the same vocabulary of named points. So the engine and the plasticity bridge port with `layer_end` set from the model's layer count. The hazards are in the conventions, listed in order of how much they change what the loop measures.

1. **Rotary positions change the map the loop iterates.** In GPT-2, the injection at layer 0 overwrites the token and position embeddings, so from iteration 1 onward the prompt survives only as a sequence length; this is established in the bridge's documentation and in the engine. In a rotary model, position is re-applied inside every attention layer, so the iterated map stays position-aware forever. Pythia already samples this side, with rotary encoding on a quarter of each head's dimensions. It is speculation, to be tested on the first run, that the position collapse this project observes on GPT-2 (all positions converging to one vector by about iteration 10) does not occur in a rotary model.

2. **The loudness convention will land somewhere new.** This project rescales the fed-back tensor to the size the stream had at the start, which for GPT-2 Small is 67 to 72 times the model's natural entry strength and for GPT-2 Medium's full stack about 218 times, and the Stage 2 operator report established that Medium's founding collapse to the letter "D" vanishes at natural strength. RMSNorm models grow their residual sizes sharply with depth, and the literature (Sun and colleagues, 2024, "Massive Activations in Large Language Models") reports activations at the first token that are hundreds to thousands of times larger than elsewhere in Llama-class models. That the small Qwen and Gemma models show the same is inferred, not checked here; the reference lens code skips the first 16 positions of every prompt for exactly this reason. Two consequences follow. A whole-tensor rescale, one that uses the Frobenius norm, the square root of the sum of squares of every number in the tensor across all positions, would be dominated by position 0. And the ratio of the fixed size to natural strength will differ from GPT-2's by an unknown factor. The recommendation is to run any new model at natural loudness, with position 0 excluded from the norm, from the first run rather than as a late control.

3. **Tokenisation and the start token.** The recorded caveat 17 (GPT-2 prepends a beginning-of-sequence token by default and Pythia does not, so position 0 is a different kind of token in the two arms) recurs: Gemma and Llama add a start token, Qwen does not. Vocabularies are three to five times larger, so every readout table and every lens dictionary grows in proportion.

4. **Gated feed-forward blocks and the plasticity site.** The plasticity module in `ATR_plasticity` attaches its Oja rule, the local learning rule that nudges one weight matrix from the activity passing through it, to `blocks.{L}.mlp.W_out` and captures the input to that matrix at `blocks.{L}.mlp.hook_post`. In the gated block of TransformerLens, `W_out` is still a bare parameter of the same shape convention and `hook_post` is still the tensor that feeds it (the product of the gate and the up-projection), established from the library source, so the site should attach unchanged. Whether the newer bridge loader also exposes `W_out` as a bare parameter that can be overwritten in place was not checked and must be before the Oja rule is trusted on it.

5. **Chat template against bare text.** A chat model expects its input wrapped in special turn-marker tokens. After iteration 0 the wrapping survives only as positions and length, so in a rotary model the two regimes differ through position alone. Run both and record which was used.

6. **Precision.** These models ship in bfloat16, a 16-bit number format with 8 bits of precision. The plasticity suite's control C0 requires bit-exact reproduction of the parent loop, and the loop runs hundreds of passes, so run in 32-bit. A one-billion-parameter model in 32-bit is 4 gigabytes of weights, which fits this project's CPU workflow, but each iteration costs roughly eight times a GPT-2 Small pass.

The recommendation is Gemma-3-270M first and Gemma-3-1B second. Both load in TransformerLens, both have pre-fitted lenses for base and instruction-tuned forms, and both are small enough to run on the CPU workflow this project already uses. Qwen3-0.6B is the natural third, as the smallest model with a trained "thinking" mode, but its lens would have to be fitted in-house.

## 5. The J-space: where the thoughts sit, how they were found, and what transfers to GPT-2

### 5.1 The rugby example as the paper actually runs it

The question recalled an example in which adding "thoughts" of rugby changes the answer to a question about a ball's shape. That fuses two of the paper's experiments. The sport example asks Claude Sonnet 4.5 to think of a sport and name it in one word, and reads the lens at the colon just before the answer; the lens reads Soccer; the intervention subtracts the Soccer lens vector and adds the Rugby lens vector at every position; the model then answers Rugby. There is no question about a ball's shape anywhere in the paper. The design in which one swapped concept feeds several different downstream questions is the France-to-China experiment: the same swap redirects "the capital of France is", "most people in France speak", "France is on the continent of" and so on, sixteen functions across four categories of concept. The remembered ball-shape version is that second design applied to the first example, and it is a good experiment: it needs only completion prompts, so it runs on a base model, including GPT-2 Small.

### 5.2 Where the directions sit relative to the residual flow

The residual stream is the vector of numbers, 768 per token position in GPT-2 Small, that each layer reads, adds to, and passes on; the unembedding matrix `W_U` turns the final layer's vector into a score for every vocabulary token. A J-lens vector for token v at layer l is row v of the product of `W_U` with `J_l`, where `J_l` is the Jacobian (the matrix of sensitivities of every output number to every input number) of the final-layer residual with respect to the layer-l residual, averaged over 1,000 web-text prompts of 128 tokens, over every source position, and over every target position at or after the source. In ordinary words: it is the direction in the layer-l residual stream along which a small push most raises the model's disposition to say token v, now or at any later position, averaged over contexts. This is established from the paper and its reference code.

Three consequences answer the question of position.

First, nothing here is orthogonal to the residual flow. The lens vectors are directions inside the ordinary residual stream at the ordinary layers, and the J-space is the set of points reachable as a sparse combination of about 25 of them with non-negative weights. It takes up at most 10 percent of the stream's variance at any layer. The "orthogonal" part is everything else, the other 90 percent or more, and the paper's swap intervention is defined so as to leave it untouched: it reads a state's coordinates on the two lens vectors involved, exchanges them, and writes the state back. A concept's activation vector keeps a median of only 6 to 7 percent of its variance inside the J-space, yet swapping that slice succeeds 59 percent of the time against 5 percent for swapping the remaining 93 percent, and a control that clamps the J-space coordinates shows that even that 5 percent routes back through the J-space. This is established.

Second, in depth the workspace band is the middle of the model: layers 38 to 92 on the paper's reindexing of every model to a 0-to-100 scale. The first third of the stack reads as nearly empty, which the paper says may be real or may be the lens's blind spot, and the last few layers are a "motor" regime whose directions are already the next-token directions. For a 12-layer GPT-2 Small that band maps to at most layers 5 to 11, an inference from proportion that the coarse depth resolution of a small model makes approximate.

Third, the reason the ordinary readout cannot see these directions at mid-depth is that their path to the output is indirect. The logit lens, which this project's readout is, applies `ln_final` and `W_U` to a mid-layer state directly; it is the same formula as the J-lens with `J_l` set to the identity. At mid-depth the direct path is weak, and the concept directions only become output directions after the downstream feed-forward blocks amplify them by about ten times, against about one times for control directions, and after a set of attention heads amounting to about one percent of those in the band carries them between positions. The paper calls these broadcast heads and shows that removing them cuts the model's ability to report an injected thought from 54 percent of trials to 9 percent while barely changing ordinary next-token prediction. This is established. So the thoughts are not hidden in a separate channel. They are residual directions whose effect on the output is deferred, and the averaged Jacobian is what makes the deferred path visible.

In the founding analogy: the lens does not find a second room. It finds which of the room's resonant directions the exit door will eventually pass, by measuring the door's response to every direction at once. The analogy stops holding at the averaging, because a room's response does not depend on what was said in it and the model's does; the paper averages over a thousand contexts precisely to wash that dependence out.

### 5.3 How they were found

The authors did not set out to find a workspace. They asked which internal representations are verbalizable, meaning poised to be spoken about should the occasion arise, and built the averaged Jacobian to answer that question, on the reasoning that a representation the model could report must have a linear route to the output that survives averaging over contexts. The computation is one forward pass plus, for each prompt, as many backward passes as the width divided by a batch dimension; the reference code confirms this and says that about 100 prompts already give a usable lens, with quality saturating quickly. Only then did they test the resulting set against five properties borrowed from global workspace theory, the cognitive-science picture of a small broadcast stage over a large unconscious backstage: verbal report, directed modulation, internal reasoning, flexible generalisation, and selectivity. The tests were causal, by swap, injection, clamp and ablation. The paper's own words are that the set satisfied all five "rather surprisingly". Two measurements that do not use the lens at all corroborate the band: a weights-only analysis of which directions compose broadly with downstream layers, and an "ignition" experiment in which the model's reading of an ambiguous input snaps to one interpretation at the layer where the band begins. This is established from the paper.

### 5.4 What transfers to GPT-2, and what this project has already measured

Established, from this project's own record:

- The reference implementation is written against the Hugging Face transformers library, not TransformerLens, and runs on GPT-2 unchanged; the Stage 2 repository fitted it to GPT-2 Medium on 100 prompts from WikiText, a corpus of Wikipedia articles, in 7 hours and 4 minutes of CPU time.
- That Medium lens passed its validation gate only marginally (clearly better than the logit lens at content positions, not better than it at prediction positions below about layer 15), and the band census found no coherent band: 0 of 23 layers met the pre-registered dominance rule, and layers 8, 9 and 10 were indistinguishable from each other and from their neighbours under every metric the lens records.
- The Stage 1 pilot on GPT-2 Small (finding F11) used a 193-token dictionary, 0.4 percent of the vocabulary, over 30 hand-written prompts, and did not support the prediction that the `prolet` attractor sits inside the verbalizable directions and the `Divine` state outside. What it found instead was a language-against-noise boundary: converged states from real prompts hold a sparse-combination share of 0.08 to 0.12, at or above their random controls, while converged noise states hold 0.05 to 0.06, below their controls of 0.08 to 0.10.
- The phase-aware re-probe (finding F16) found that the physical flip axis of the `Divine` cycle lies almost wholly outside the lens: a span share of 0.013 at layer 11 against a chance level of 0.252, about 5 percent of what a random direction would score. The cycle's two phases straddle the `prolet` level, and its pivot is the most lens-expressible state probed.

Inferred: the mechanism series' conclusion that the cycle rides an axis outside both the readout and the lens is consistent with the paper's own finding that over 90 percent of activation variance lies outside the J-space. The loop's dynamics live mostly in the silent machinery, and the decoded word is what the exit door reports of them.

Speculation, flagged as such by the paper itself: a 124-million-parameter base model may have no organised workspace at all, since the paper leaves open when the workspace emerges in training and studied nothing smaller than Claude Haiku 4.5. The paper's figure on model size shows the "think about X" instruction loading X into the lens more often as the model grows from Haiku to Sonnet to Opus, which suggests, without establishing, that the instruction-dependent experiments (directed modulation, injected-thought report, the white-bear effect in which an instruction to ignore X loads X) will fail below about a billion parameters even on a chat-trained Qwen or Gemma.

This sorts the paper's experiments into two classes for this project. The completion-compatible ones need only a prompt that ends where an answer begins: the report swap at a colon or after "is", flexible generalisation across several questions about one swapped concept, and the intermediate-step surgery in which spider becomes ant and the legs count changes from eight to six. These run on base GPT-2 and on this project's terminal states. The instruction-dependent ones need a model that can be told what to think about while doing something else, and enough capability to comply, and those are the ones a small chat model may or may not deliver.

### 5.5 A pre-fitted lens for GPT-2 Small now exists

The Hugging Face repository `neuronpedia/jacobian-lens` publishes fitted lenses for 36 models, and on the day of writing its file listing includes `gpt2-small/jlens/Salesforce-wikitext/gpt2_jacobian_lens.pt` at 12,980,477 bytes, with a `config.yaml` and a convergence table beside it. The folder name records WikiText as the fitting corpus, the same corpus the Stage 2 Medium fit used; the prompt count is not in the filename and should be read from the configuration file. The file size is consistent with eleven source layers of 768 by 768 numbers in 16-bit precision, that is, a lens from every layer's output to the final block; that reading is inferred from the byte count, not from documentation. Neuronpedia's write-up adds one observation about small models that is relevant to any swap experiment on GPT-2: "for smaller models, we noticed that it was easy to oversteer swaps, it required selecting fewer layers to swap to the desired result".

Loading it is two lines with the reference package installed:

```python
model = jlens.from_hf(hf_model, tokenizer)
lens = jlens.JacobianLens.from_pretrained(
    "neuronpedia/jacobian-lens",
    filename="gpt2-small/jlens/Salesforce-wikitext/gpt2_jacobian_lens.pt")
```

The listing also holds lenses for `pythia-70m-deduped`, `gemma-2-2b` and `gemma-2-2b-it`, `gemma-3-270m` and `gemma-3-270m-it`, and `gemma-3-1b` and `gemma-3-1b-it`, which is why section 4 recommends the Gemma 3 models as the first modern port. There is no lens for GPT-2 Medium, Pythia-160m or Pythia-410m.

## 6. What remains, and what needs the operator's decision

What happened: four questions were answered from the record and the literature, and one new fact was found, the pre-fitted GPT-2 Small lens. What it means: the planned overlap test on Small (experiment EXP_011, hypothesis H6 in the Stage 2 register, which asks whether the five basin tensors project more onto the verbalizable directions than the null-model basins do) can run against the paper's own full-vocabulary instrument instead of the 0.4-percent pilot dictionary, and the first modern-model port has an obvious candidate.

What remains, in order of information per unit cost, none of it started:

1. Run the Small overlap test with the pre-fitted lens, phase-aware as finding F16 requires (both phases of the `Divine` cycle and its pivot), against the same size-and-norm-matched random dictionaries the pilot used. Cost: under a day, no lens fitting.
2. Run the completion-compatible swap experiments on base GPT-2 Small: the sport swap at a colon, the ball-shape follow-up as a flexible-generalisation test, and the spider-to-ant surgery. Cost: a day, and it would be the first causal test of whether GPT-2 Small's lens directions do anything.
3. Load LaMini-GPT-124M through the existing `hf_model=` route and repeat the loop and the overlap test on it, as the base-against-post-trained comparison. Cost: a day.
4. Port the loop to Gemma-3-270M under natural loudness with position 0 excluded from the norm, and run the same overlap test with its pre-fitted lens. Cost: two to three days, most of it in checking the conventions listed in section 4.2.
5. Open the Coconut line: fine-tune GPT-2 Small with the public Coconut code and run the loop and lens on the result. Cost: a GPU day for the fine-tune, then the same analyses.

What needs the operator's decision:

- Whether the Small overlap test may use a third-party-fitted lens as its instrument, or must use one fitted in-house from a committed corpus. The register's discipline is that the instrument is part of the pre-registration, and a downloaded file has a provenance the project does not control; the Medium fit's checkpoint, by contrast, was never committed either. Either choice is defensible, and the choice should be written into the specification before the run.
- Whether Gemma-3-270M is the first modern port, and under which loudness convention, since the Stage 2 record now treats the loudness convention as a load-bearing apparatus parameter rather than a detail.
- Whether the Coconut line is opened as an experiment of its own. Under the Stage 2 rules it needs an experiment identifier claimed in the register before any specification uses it.

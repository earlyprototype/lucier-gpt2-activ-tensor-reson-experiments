Six ways of dissolving

Feed a sentence back through a language model until it stops changing, and track every word it passes through on the way. Time runs left to right; the stack is how many distinct words are still alive. All six runs below use the same procedure. What they leave behind could hardly be more different.

Start with GPT-2 Small. Fifty-three distinct words at the first pass, five at the last, and those five are the basins this whole project is named after: prolet, Divine, Anarch, till, solidarity. Seventy prompts about physics, weather, medicine and nursery rhymes, and they end in one of five words.

Now press GPT-2 Medium. Fifty-two words down to one. Every single prompt ends at the letter D. The larger model of the same family does not find five rooms; it finds one, and everything falls into it.

Pythia 160M is nearly the same story with a different word: questioned takes 93 per cent of everything. Then press Pythia 410M and the pattern breaks completely. It starts at 45 distinct words and ends at 34. It never consolidates. Run it ten times longer, as the deep arm does, and it still does not. One model in this set simply refuses to settle.

Finally the control, which is noise fed through the same loop instead of sentences. It collapses too, mostly onto a typographic dash. So collapse by itself proves nothing. It is not evidence that the model is doing something semantic, and anyone who reads a funnel as meaning must reckon with the fact that random tensors funnel as well. What differs between these six is not whether they collapse. It is how far, how fast, and onto what.

Reading

Horizontal position is the pass number, read straight from the archive. Vertical position is a drawing choice: words are stacked by how many prompts hold them, sorted so a strand keeps its neighbours, then relaxed to reduce crossings. Stack height is therefore the surviving population and is the one vertical fact worth reading.

Passes sampled are 0, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100 and beyond, so the horizontal axis is an ordering of observations, not a clock.

Colour is the rank of the ending, since the runs do not share a vocabulary. Grey led somewhere outside the top five endings of that run.

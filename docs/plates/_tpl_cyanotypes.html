Every attention head in this model was set running on its own, fed its own output over and over, and watched until it settled. What it settled into has a spectrum, and that spectrum is drawn here as a form: the leading modes set the lobes, the tail sets the fineness of the edge, and the strength of the head's dominant direction sets the phase of every lobe. One head, one specimen. Nothing is stylised; two heads look alike when their spectra are alike.

They sort themselves into four kinds, and the sorting was done by earlier measurement rather than by eye. Fifty-two settle onto a single point and stay. Fifty-seven rotate, turning forever without arriving. Twenty-nine flip sign every pass, which is the same two-beat oscillation that drives the one cycle we have studied closely elsewhere in this work. Six are still slowly moving when the observation stops.

One specimen does not belong, and you will find it without being told: last row, ninth along. That is layer 11, head 8. Where every other head opens into spikes, it stays closed and dense, and it is rank one of 144 on that measure, the least spiky form on the plate.

Three separate measurements meet on that one square, and none of them knew about the others. Its leading eigenvalue is 86.7, the largest of all 144, where the next-largest is a small fraction of it and the median sits near one. It is classified a sign-flipper, which is the two-beat oscillation that drives the one limit cycle we have studied in detail. And it is the head that, in the full feedback loop with all 144 running together, contributes most of the per-pass turn at every injection level we tested. A spectral census, a stability class, and an attribution experiment, all pointing at the same square of the plate.

The halo marks a head whose leading eigenvalue exceeds one, so a disturbance along its dominant direction grows rather than shrinks. There are far more of those than I expected, which is worth sitting with: most of these parts, run alone, do not settle down.

Process

After Anna Atkins, Photographs of British Algae: Cyanotype Impressions, 1843, the first book illustrated by photography and a book of specimens. White where the light was blocked, Prussian blue where it was not.

Honesty

Each head was looped in isolation, which is not what any head does inside the working model. These are portraits of parts removed from the whole. The class labels come from the earlier measurement and are reproduced, not recomputed. The mapping from spectrum to outline is a drawing convention; the spectra are not.

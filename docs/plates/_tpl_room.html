Three plates from a room made of weights

I am feeding a sentence into a language model and stopping it halfway, before it has said anything. What I take out is not a word but a state: some seven thousand numbers, the shape the sentence has made inside the machine. I set that state to a chosen loudness and feed it back in as though it were the sentence. Then again. Then again, some hundreds of times. What the sentence said stops mattering rather quickly. What survives is whatever the room resonates at. These are pictures of the room.
Plate I

Where every run ended

Reading it   each dot is one run's final state; nearby dots are similar states, far-apart dots different ones. A lit colour means that run settled on one of the five words; grey means it ended anywhere else. Drag to turn the cloud.

Every one of the 1,425 runs, each a single point placed where it came to rest. Five of these resting places are lit. Those five are the whole finding: across hundreds of unrelated prompts, at the right loudness, the loop lands on one of five words and no others. Everything unlit ended somewhere outside that set, and there are a great many of those. Turn the loudness colouring on and the cloud reorganises: the lit five occupy a band in the middle, with the failures stacked below and above it.

Plate II

Sixty passes, four architectures

Reading it   one line per sentence, one step per pass through the model; the bright dot is where that sentence's state is right now. The five start scattered and, where a model converges, arrive at one spot and stop.

The settling itself, drawn one pass at a time. Each trace is one prompt; the head of the trace is where the state is now. They begin scattered and end arriving. This is the part that is genuinely like the tape piece: the opening passes still carry the sentence, and by the end the trace has stopped moving because nothing is left but the resonance. Four different models are shown, two families and two sizes. They are not measured against each other here, only put side by side.

Plate III

The band, as a curve

Reading it   one node per loudness setting, joined in order from 1x to 1024x the natural level. Height is how much a single pass grows the state; depth is how far a pass turns it. The big lit nodes are the settings where most runs end in the five words.

The knob itself, all twenty-one settings, drawn as one continuous path. Left to right is loudness. The two other axes are the only two things a pass can do to a state: change its size, and change its direction. The path rises, arcs over, and falls away, and the five-word band sits exactly on the crest. Nodes swell where more of the runs landed in the five. Note the far end: the curve flattens onto the floor, because at that loudness the loop has stopped doing anything at all and simply hands back what it was given.

On what these pictures are not

The states live in roughly seven thousand dimensions. Every plate here is a shadow cast into three, chosen so that the shadow keeps as much of the motion as possible. Plate I keeps 94.7 per cent of it, which is unusually generous and means the clustering you see is close to real. Plate II keeps between 76 and 98 per cent depending on the model, so the Pythia 410M traces are the least trustworthy of the four.

A shadow can still flatter. Two points that look adjacent may be far apart along the discarded directions, and no rotation of these plates will reveal it. Nothing here is evidence on its own; the numbers behind it are.

The five lit words are the ones the loop settles on inside the band. What sets the edges of that band is an open question, and an earlier claim to have answered it was withdrawn.

Drawn from committed run archives, no new computation.

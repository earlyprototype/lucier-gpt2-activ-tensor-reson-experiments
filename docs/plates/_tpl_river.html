Six ways of dissolving

A sentence's internal state is fed back through a language model, pass after pass, and the one word the model would say is recorded at each pass. The procedure is repeated for many prompts at once and the runs are stacked: each bar in the stack is one word, the number of separate bars is the number of distinct words the runs hold between them, and time runs left to right. All six arms below use the same procedure. What remains at the last pass differs from arm to arm, from five words, to one, to no consolidation at all.

GPT-2 Small: 53 distinct words at the first pass, five at the last, and those five are the five basin words of finding F1: prolet, Divine, Anarch, till, solidarity. The arm tracks 70 prompts about physics, weather, medicine and nursery rhymes, and every one ends in one of the five. A limit applies to every model arm on this page: each draws the 70 runs, one per prompt, whose full word-by-word pathways were archived out of that model's 125-prompt sweep, so every share here is a share of those 70. The findings log's canonical count, taken over all 125 prompts at lock-in, puts prolet at 43.2 per cent where these 70 show 38.6.

GPT-2 Medium: 52 distinct words down to one. All 70 runs end at the letter D. The larger model of the same family does not produce five basins; it produces one, and everything falls into it.

Pythia 160M is nearly the same shape with a different word: questioned takes 65 of the arm's 70 runs, about 93 per cent, and two other words, sight and asked, hold the remaining five. Pythia 410M breaks the pattern. Its 70 runs start spread across 45 distinct words, narrow to 19 by pass 2, scatter back to 40 by pass 20, and end on 34 at pass 250; the count never consolidates. The deep arm carries eight of its runs to pass 1,000, four times the span of the main panel, and at the last pass the eight runs hold eight different words. One model in the set does not settle.

The control is noise fed through the same loop in place of sentences: 20 of its 125 trials are drawn, and 14 of the 20 collapse onto a typographic dash. One correction stands on this panel: it draws the original noise arm, kept as the historical record, which was later found to have been injected more weakly than the language runs. The strength-matched re-run also collapses, and lands mostly in the language arm's own five basins. Collapse by itself is therefore not evidence of anything semantic: random tensors funnel as well. What differs across the six arms is not whether they collapse but how far, how fast, and onto what.

Reading

Each arm tracks a fixed set of runs, one per prompt, and every run holds exactly one word at every pass, so the population never grows or shrinks. Each vertical bar is one word, its height the number of runs holding that word at that pass; a strand's thickness is the number of runs making that word-to-word step. The total inked thickness in a column is therefore constant within an arm. What changes is the number of separate bars, the count of distinct words the runs are spread across: a column that sprouts more bars is fragmenting, not gaining population. Vertical order is a drawing choice: bars are sorted so a strand keeps its neighbours, then relaxed to reduce crossings.

Horizontal position is the order of the sampled passes, evenly spaced, so the axis is an ordering of observations, not a clock. The 100-pass arms sample passes 0, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 50 and 100; the long Pythia-410m arm continues to 150, 200 and 250; the deep arm samples 0, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 200, 300, 500, 750 and 1,000.

Colour follows the rank of the ending word within its own arm, counted by how many runs finish on it: amber for the largest share, then flame, blue, teal and pale. The six arms do not share a vocabulary, so a colour never means the same word in two arms. Grey marks a path that led outside the arm's top five endings, and grey strands are drawn fainter. In the deep arm all eight endings hold one run each, so its five colours are an arbitrary tie-break. Brighter ticks in the final column are the ending words themselves; the right edge names at most the fourteen topmost survivors, so the Pythia-410m arm's 34 endings are not all labelled.

Each of the 144 attention heads in GPT-2 Small was run in isolation: the head was applied to its own output repeatedly and observed for 500 passes. The form in each square is drawn from that head's weights. A head stretches some directions of the model's internal space more than others, and the ordered list of those stretch factors, strongest first, is the head's spectrum. The first twenty stretch factors after the strongest set the lobes of the form, the tail of smaller ones sets the fineness of the edge, and the head's dominant direction, the one it stretches most, sets where every lobe falls. The mapping from spectrum to form is fixed and identical for all 144 heads, so two forms agree only when both the spectra and the dominant directions agree. Every form is scaled to fill its own square; size therefore carries no information on this plate, only shape does.

The heads fall into four classes, assigned by prior measurement rather than by eye: 52 of the 144 are fixed-point, 57 rotating, 29 sign-flip and 6 slow-converging. The legend beneath the plate defines each class and its centre mark, and each legend entry filters the plate.

One specimen is visibly unlike the other 143: layer 11, head 8, in the bottom row, ninth column. Where other forms open into spikes, it stays closed and dense. Spikes are the lesser stretch factors drawn relative to the strongest, so a closed form means one stretch factor far above all the rest. For this head the strongest stretch is 11.5 times the second; the runner-up across the plate reaches 3.6, and the median head sits at 1.2. On this measure the head ranks first of 144.

Three independent measurements meet on that square. The head's leading eigenvalue, the factor by which one pass grows or shrinks a disturbance along its dominant direction, has size 86.7, the largest of all 144; the next largest is 9.6 and the median is 1.9. The eigenvalue's sign is negative, which is why the head is classified a sign-flipper: its state lands on one axis and reverses along it every pass. In the separate attribution experiment on the full model, with all 144 heads running together, the same head carries 99 per cent of the attention contribution to the direction reversal that drives the two-beat limit cycle documented in the findings log, and removing the head collapses that cycle. A spectral census, a stability classification and an attribution experiment converge on the same square.

113 of the 144 heads carry the halo, a leading eigenvalue size above one. Growth along one direction does not prevent settling: the iteration holds the state's overall size fixed, and 45 of the 52 fixed-point heads carry the halo yet settle in direction. The halo records that without that constraint, motion along the dominant direction would diverge.

Marks

The figure at a specimen's centre is its class mark, defined in the legend above the text. For a rotating head the arc's sweep is the fraction of the head's turning that stays in the flat plane its own wiring predicts: all but two of the 57 rotating heads earn the full circle, layer 0 head 1 shows about half, and layer 6 head 8 falls just short. The gold ring outside a form is the halo. A class switched off in the legend stays in place but fades.

Limitations

Each head was looped in isolation, with none of the machinery that surrounds it in the working model: no attention weighting over a real context, no re-balancing of the residual stream, no bias terms. The plate therefore claims nothing about heads at work together. Observation stopped at pass 500. The class labels come from the earlier census and are reproduced here, not recomputed. The mapping from spectrum to outline is a drawing convention, and the four nested contour lines inside each form are shading, not data; the spectra themselves are measured. The token in the readout is what the head's dominant direction spells when pushed directly to the vocabulary; it is a property of the weights, not of the settled state, and for most heads the settled state points elsewhere (median agreement between the two directions is 0.16 on a scale of 0 to 1).

# Does WebText carry the traffic of non-western influence operations?

**A provenance audit of GPT-2's training corpus.** 260,000 documents scanned against
77 indicators drawn from public attributions; 605 document-indicator pairs classified;
213 adjudicated by hand.

> **Verdict in one line.** The hypothesis as posed — that WebText contains *evidence of
> non-western asymmetric warfare via botfarming* — is **not supported**, and one of its two
> halves is **refuted**. What the corpus does contain is **155 documents of state-produced
> material (5.96 per 10,000)**, arriving overwhelmingly through the *overt* broadcast arm
> rather than the covert one, and **RT ranked 92nd of all domains in WebText** — ahead of
> Al Jazeera. The corpus ingested state media. It did not, on this evidence, ingest a bot farm.

---

## 1. What was actually asked, and what can actually be tested

The hypothesis bundles two claims that have to be separated, because the corpus can speak
to one and is nearly blind to the other.

| | Claim | Testable here? |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Content** | Material produced by non-western state influence operations is present in WebText | **Yes.** The text is the evidence. |
| **(b) Mechanism** | It got there by botfarming — karma farming, brigading, coordinated inauthentic upvoting | **Almost entirely no.** See below. |

WebText was built by scraping outbound links from Reddit posts with **at least 3 karma**
(Radford et al. 2019, §2.1). The published artifacts are the *documents* and a *domain
census*. Reddit-side signals — who submitted, from which account, with what vote timing,
in which subreddit — **were never released and are not recoverable from the corpus**.
A karma-farming claim is a claim about vote provenance, and vote provenance is exactly
what the released data omits.

So this study tests (a) directly, tests what (b) leaves behind *in the text* (republication
chains, duplicate copy), and is explicit that the rest of (b) is out of reach. That
asymmetry is the single most important thing to carry out of this document: the 3-karma
gate is a very low bar — a handful of votes — and clearing it needs no bot farm at all.

### The silly part, kept honest

This was commissioned as "something slightly silly". The silliness is in the framing —
running a counter-influence-operations regime over a 2019 language-model corpus — not in
the method. Every indicator carries a public-record attribution, every headline number is
regenerated by a script in this directory, and the result is reported as it came out,
including the parts that refute the premise.

## 2. Method

*Summarised here; the full protocol — indicator inclusion rules, the adjudication procedure,
threats to validity, and how to port the regime to another corpus — is in
[METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md).*

Six scripts, run in order. Raw corpus files are gitignored (692 MB); everything needed to
audit a claim is committed.

| Script | Does |
|---|---|
| `01_fetch_corpus.py` | Fetches the corpus and census, verifies SHA-256 against pinned values |
| `02_scan_corpus.py` | Streams 260k documents against all 77 indicators |
| `03_classify_hits.py` | Origin-vs-mention classification; merges hand adjudications |
| `04_near_duplicates.py` | Shingle-based near-duplicate clustering across hit documents |
| `05_domain_census.py` | Cross-references OpenAI's whole-corpus domain census |
| `06_headline_stats.py` | Aggregates every figure quoted below |

**Corpus.** OpenAI's `gpt-2-output-dataset` release of original WebText: 250,000 + 5,000 +
5,000 = **260,000 documents, 152.2M byte-pair-encoding tokens, 680 MB of text**. This is
the only public sample of WebText, drawn from the held-out portion of the same scrape that
produced the training split. Plus `domains.txt` from `openai/gpt-2`: the top 1,000 domains
across the **whole** corpus, training split included.

**Indicators** (`indicators.json`) are tiered by *evidence class*, not severity:

- **Tier A — attributed covert assets** (27). Sites and personas named by the U.S. Department
  of Justice's Internet Research Agency indictment, U.S. Treasury sanctions designations
  (SouthFront/FSB, Strategic Culture Foundation/SVR, InfoRos/GRU), the U.S. State
  Department Global Engagement Center's *Pillars* report, FireEye's Iranian Liberty Front
  Press attribution, and the New Knowledge report to the Senate Select Committee on
  Intelligence. **These are the hypothesis's real test**: covert assets had little organic
  audience and documented inauthentic distribution.
- **Tier B — overt state media** (27). RT, Sputnik, Press TV, Xinhua, China Daily, CCTV,
  People's Daily, TASS, Telesur, KCNA. Openly state-owned; presence shows the pipeline
  admitted state content, not that its karma was inauthentic.
- **Tier C — documented amplifiers** (3). Global Research, Veterans Today, 21st Century Wire.
- **Tier M — meta-discussion markers** (9). "Internet Research Agency", "troll farm",
  "Savushkina", "50 Cent Party" — measuring how much the corpus talks *about* the war.
- **Tier BASE — baseline comparators** (11), including Al Jazeera as a deliberate
  state-funded-but-normalized contrast.

**Matching** is boundary-aware, unit-tested in `tests/test_webtext_provenance_scanlib.py`
(7 tests). A domain indicator matches when its pattern is the *registrable tail* of an
extracted domain token: `www.rt.com` and `amp.rt.com` hit `rt.com`; `support.com` and
`rt.com.au` do not.

### The classification that carries the study

Keyword counting cannot distinguish a corpus **full of BBC articles about the Internet
Research Agency** from a corpus **containing Internet Research Agency articles**. The first
is coverage; the second is ingestion. Every pair therefore receives one of seven verdicts:

`origin` · `laundered_origin` (state copy carried in via a repost — *"originally posted at
strategic-culture.org"*) · `citation_amplification` (third party building on the source) ·
`wire_carriage` (third party carrying wire copy with attribution) · `mention` ·
`false_positive` · `ambiguous`

**Every Tier A pair, every Tier B and C domain pair, and every pair the heuristics could not
settle was read by hand.** 213 of 605 pairs carry a recorded adjudication with quoted
evidence in `output/adjudications.json`. **Zero pairs remain `ambiguous`.**

## 3. Results

### 3.1 The covert tier is empty — and that is the finding

**18 of 19 attributed covert-asset domains return zero documents.** Not low counts: zero.

BlackMattersUS, BlackToLive, DoNotShoot.us, Black4Black, SouthFront, News Front, InfoRos,
New Eastern Outlook, Katehon, Geopolitica, RIA FAN, PolitExpert, Liberty Front Press,
AWD News, IUVM Press, WhatsUpic, Real Progressive Front, British Left — **all absent from
260,000 documents.**

Persona names fare the same. `TEN_GOP`, `Jenna Abrams`, `Being Patriotic`, `Secured Borders`,
`Army of Jesus`, `United Muslims of America`: zero. `Blacktivist` appears **once** — in a
news report about Twitter's congressional testimony. `Heart of Texas` appears three times:
one BBC report on Facebook's disclosure, one Texas prison-ministry charity, and the 1941
song *Deep in the Heart of Texas*.

The single covert asset that is present is **Strategic Culture Foundation** — the outlet the
U.S. Treasury designated in April 2021 as directed by Russia's Foreign Intelligence Service
(SVR). It appears in **four documents**, all four the same author (Eric Zuesse), all four
carrying an explicit repost header:

> *"Eric Zuesse, originally posted at strategic-culture.org"*
> *"Submitted by Eric Zuesse, via strategic-culture.org"*

One of the four documents narrates its own laundering path: it notes the piece was submitted
to "virtually all U.S. national 'news' media" and that "the only U.S.-based news-medium that
published the article was Zero Hedge." That is content laundering visible in the text, and
it is the strongest single piece of evidence this study found. It is also **four documents
out of 260,000**.

| Tier A outcome | Documents |
|---|---|
| State-produced (all `laundered_origin`, all Strategic Culture) | **4** |
| Press coverage *about* the operations (`mention`) | 2 |
| False positives (song title, prison charity) | 2 |
| **Covert-asset domains with zero hits** | **18 of 19** |

### 3.2 The overt tier is a different story

| Actor | State-produced documents |
|---|---|
| Russia / Sputnik | **74** |
| China / Xinhua | 42 |
| Iran / Press TV | 16 |
| Russia / RT | 9 |
| China / China Daily | 8 |
| Russia / Strategic Culture Foundation (Tier A) | 4 |
| China / CCTV | 2 |
| **Total distinct documents** | **155** (5.96 per 10,000) |

These are not mentions. They are Sputnik wire copy carrying `MOSCOW (Sputnik) —` datelines
and `© Sputnik /` photo furniture; Xinhua dispatches opening `BEIJING, Jan. 16 (Xinhua) --`;
Press TV interview transcripts opening *"Press TV has conducted an interview with…"*; China
Daily articles with reporter contacts at `@chinadaily.com.cn`; CCTV pages headed
`Editor: Li Kun 丨CCTV.com`.

**19 documents are laundered** — state copy rehosted elsewhere with a credit line, the same
pattern as the Strategic Culture cluster: `ORIGINALLY PUBLISHED AT RT.COM`, `Rt.com reports:`,
`Contributed by RT.com of RT.com`, `Press TV original title:`. A further **25 documents**
are third-party pieces built on state-media sourcing.

### 3.3 The census result: RT is the 92nd most-linked domain in WebText

The text sample can only see documents that name their own source. `domains.txt` has no such
limitation — it covers the entire corpus. Its counts sum to 21.8M against a corpus of ~8M
documents, so it counts **links** among the ~45M scraped, not final documents.

| Domain | Tier | Rank | Links |
|---|---|---|---|
| nytimes | BASE | 5 | 333,160 |
| bbc | BASE | 9 | 311,917 |
| theguardian | BASE | 10 | 246,303 |
| cnn | BASE | 13 | 199,360 |
| foxnews | BASE | 35 | 79,494 |
| breitbart | BASE | 44 | 71,743 |
| **rt** | **B** | **92** | **38,575** |
| aljazeera | BASE | 112 | 33,867 |
| sputniknews | B | 346 | 12,611 |
| presstv | B | 666 | 7,122 |
| globalresearch | C | 736 | 6,424 |
| telesurtv | B | 950 | 5,137 |

**RT out-ranks Al Jazeera in Reddit's outbound link graph.** Four non-western state outlets
and one documented amplifier sit inside the top 1,000 domains of GPT-2's training corpus.

And yet: Tier B is **0.29%** of top-1000 links and Tier C is **0.029%**, against **8.69%**
for the eleven baseline outlets. State media is *present and unmistakable*, and *small*.

**No attributed covert asset appears anywhere in the top 1,000.** The census and the text
scan agree, and they are near-independent measurements — one covering the whole corpus by
link, the other the sample by content.

### 3.4 The coordination check returns a clean null

If the same state-produced text entered WebText through multiple URLs, that would be
republication doing what laundering is for, and each copy would have cleared the karma gate
separately. Across all 578 non-baseline hit documents, using 8-word shingles and exact
pairwise Jaccard similarity:

**Zero pairs at similarity ≥ 0.50. Zero pairs even at the loosened ≥ 0.25.** No clusters.

The two Neil Clark author-biography documents — the closest thing to a duplicate pair, sharing
a verbatim biography paragraph — score **0.066**. Deduplication in the WebText pipeline may
account for some of this; the null is reported as measured.

### 3.5 The corpus talks about the war more coherently than it carries it

Tier M markers appear in **18 documents, all 26 pairs classified as `mention`** — reporting on
the Internet Research Agency, troll farms, and the 50 Cent Party. WebText is a corpus that
*covered* influence operations. Meanwhile `Savushkina`, `Olgino`, `Glavset` and `wumao` —
the vocabulary of people who study them closely — are all **zero**.

## 4. What this does and does not support

**Refuted:** that WebText contains a meaningful body of *covert* influence-operation content.
18 of 19 attributed covert domains are absent from 260,000 documents and none appears in the
top-1,000 census. The one present asset contributes four documents by a single author.

**Not supported:** the botfarming mechanism. Not because it was tested and failed, but
because **the released data cannot test it** — Reddit-side vote provenance was never
published. The one text-visible proxy available, near-duplicate republication, returned a
clean null. A 3-karma threshold is a bar a few genuine upvotes clear.

**Supported, and worth stating plainly:** WebText ingested **non-western state media at
measurable scale through ordinary organic sharing**. 155 documents of state-produced copy;
RT the 92nd-ranked domain, ahead of Al Jazeera; 19 documents where state copy was laundered
through a third-party rehost.

The interesting finding is the *shape*, not the size. The covert apparatus — the part built
to be amplified inauthentically — is essentially **absent**. The overt apparatus, which
never needed a bot farm because it had genuine readers who genuinely posted its links, is
**present throughout**. On this evidence WebText was not infiltrated. It was *read*.

Whether 5.96 documents per 10,000 shifted anything in GPT-2's weights is a different
question, and this study does not address it. It is a corpus audit, not a model probe.

## 5. Limitations

1. **Mechanism is out of reach.** No submitter accounts, no vote timing, no subreddit
   provenance. The central term of the hypothesis is unmeasurable from the released data.
2. **The sample is the held-out split**, not the training split. Same pipeline, unbiased
   draw, but not the exact documents GPT-2 saw. The census covers everything and is the
   corrective.
3. **Text capture is lossy and biased.** Most articles do not name their own domain. Sputnik
   scored **zero** `sputniknews.com` text hits against 12,611 census links — the capture
   came from phrase indicators instead. Real presence therefore **exceeds** every count here;
   the state-produced figure is a **floor**, not an estimate.
4. **Indicator coverage is bounded by public attribution, and by the window.** Documented
   covert Chinese English-language networks (the "Spamouflage" cluster) were reported from
   2019 — after WebText closed in December 2017. Their absence here is a scope condition,
   not a finding. Russian-language assets are near-null by construction in an
   English-dominated corpus.
5. **The census is second-level labels only.** `people` is People magazine, not People's
   Daily — flagged and excluded. `rt` is assumed to be rt.com at that volume.
6. **A conservative-classification bias exists and cuts one way.** Where evidence was
   ambiguous, the verdict went to `mention`. This makes the state-produced count an
   underestimate.
7. **Line 217 of `domains.txt` is malformed** in OpenAI's published file: a bare count
   (20,068) with no domain name. Recorded in `output/domain_census.json` under
   `census_anomalies`, included in link totals, attributable to nothing.

## 6. Reproducing

```bash
cd experiments/webtext_provenance
python3 01_fetch_corpus.py      # ~692 MB, checksum-verified, idempotent
python3 02_scan_corpus.py       # ~50 s over 260k documents
python3 03_classify_hits.py
python3 04_near_duplicates.py
python3 05_domain_census.py
python3 06_headline_stats.py
pytest ../../tests/test_webtext_provenance_scanlib.py
```

Committed outputs: `output/hits_nonbase.jsonl` (every non-baseline hit with context),
`classified_hits.json`, `adjudications.json` (213 hand verdicts with quoted evidence),
`scan_summary.json`, `classification_summary.json`, `domain_census.json`,
`dup_clusters.json`, `headline_stats.json`.

### Glossary

- **BPE (byte-pair encoding)** — the sub-word tokenisation GPT-2 uses; the corpus's `length`
  field counts these tokens.
- **Jaccard similarity** — overlap of two sets divided by their union; 1.0 is identical,
  0.0 disjoint.
- **Shingle** — a fixed-length run of consecutive words (8 here), used as the unit for
  near-duplicate comparison.
- **Karma** — Reddit's net-vote score. WebText required at least 3.
- **Content laundering** — republishing state-origin material through an intermediary so it
  reaches audiences that would not visit the source.
